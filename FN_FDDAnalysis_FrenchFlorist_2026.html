Key FDD Items — 2026 Filing
Item 5 — Initial Franchise Fee Amber
$53,000–$128,000 depending on territory type and size. Standard franchise fee on the high end for an emerging brand.
The range is wide — $53K at the low end (conversion franchise) to $128K for a premium territory. Conversion franchises (existing independent florists converting to the French Florist brand) carry a lower entry fee, which is how the brand is building its "Conversion Division." For a brand with only 17 open locations, the upper fee range sits above the retail franchise industry average of ~$37K. Multi-territory discounts not publicly confirmed.
Item 6 — Ongoing Fee Burden Green
Royalty: 6% · Brand fund: 2% · Total burden: ~8%
8% total fee burden is competitive and below the retail franchise average. The 2% brand fund supports the "marketing as a service" model where French Florist executes digital marketing centrally on behalf of franchisees — covering SEO, paid ads, and demand generation. Franchisees can dial marketing spend up or down with additional ad contributions beyond the 2% floor, per founder interviews.
Item 7 — Total Investment Range Amber
Standard: $242,675–$398,675  ·  Conversion: $200,675–$379,675
Planning amount (high end + 10% buffer): ~$438K standard, ~$418K conversion. The investment range reflects a physical retail boutique with build-out, inventory, proprietary software setup, and working capital. The conversion path is the more accessible entry — an independent florist who already has a shop, existing customer base, and equipment can convert at a materially lower cost and with day-one revenue. No franchisor financing offered directly.
Item 10 — Franchisor Financing Amber
No direct financing · Refers to third-party lenders · SBA status: verify
French Florist does not offer in-house financing. They refer candidates to financing sources but don't name preferred SBA lenders publicly. PeerSense lists First Bank of the Lake and iTHINK Financial CU as top SBA lenders for this brand, suggesting SBA eligibility — but the brand's franchisor net loss and shareholder's deficit (see Item 21) may complicate lender due diligence. Verify SBA eligibility directly with lenders before promising this pathway to candidates.
Item 11 — Ownership Model Green
E-commerce driven · 95% orders placed online · Semi-absentee friendly
The 95% online order model is a structural differentiator — the franchisor drives inbound demand through centralized digital marketing, orders route to the nearest boutique through proprietary software, and franchisees manage fulfillment. This reduces the reliance on walk-in foot traffic and makes it more compatible with semi-absentee or manager-led ownership. Training program details not fully publicly disclosed — request Item 11 specifics from franchisor.
Item 12 — Territory Rights Green
Protected territory · 16 states available including GA, TX, FL, NY, CA
Available states per franchisor site: AZ, CA, CO, CT, DE, FL, GA, LA, NV, NJ, NY, OH, OR, PA, TX, WA. Priority development in Texas, NYC metro, and Florida. Territory protection is listed as geographic but exact definition (zip, population-based, or radius) should be confirmed from the FDD. With 45 territories awarded and only 17 open, a significant gap exists between territory sales and actual openings — a flag worth monitoring.
Item 19 — Financial Performance Red
Not disclosed in FDD · Corporate location revenue cited in press only
French Florist does not include a financial performance representation (Item 19) in its FDD. All revenue figures circulating publicly — the "$800K–$900K" and "3x the industry average" claims — come from franchisor press releases and founder interviews, not from the FDD. The industry average for independent florists is ~$350K at ~10% margin. Franchisor claims corporate locations operate at 18%+ operating income after all fees. None of this is independently verified through FDD disclosure. This is the single most important data gap for any candidate evaluating this brand.
Item 20 — System Health Red
17 open locations · 45 territories awarded · 28 awarded but not yet open
The gap between territories awarded (45) and locations open (17) is worth watching. In a healthy franchise system, award-to-open lag is typically 6–12 months for retail concepts. If territories were awarded throughout 2025 and into 2026, some lag is expected. But if locations are stalling post-award — due to financing, site selection, build-out, or franchisor support capacity — that lag becomes a system health signal. PeerSense FPI score: 52/100, below average. Churn data not available due to limited system history.
Item 21 — Franchisor Financial Health Red
Net loss: $1.18M · Shareholder deficit: $1.15M · Related-party loan: $3.1M
This is the most significant flag in the entire FDD. The 2024 audited financials show the franchisor itself is operating at a loss and has a negative shareholder equity position, sustained primarily by a $3.1 million related-party loan. The $3 million strategic investment secured in September 2024 partially offsets this, and the rapid territory sales in 2025 generate franchise fee revenue. But franchise fee revenue is one-time — it cannot sustain the business long-term. Franchisor continuity risk is elevated relative to mature systems. Not a disqualifier but a serious due diligence flag that every candidate and their attorney should review.
Item 23 — Exit Rights Amber
Transfer and renewal terms · Not publicly itemized · Request from franchisor
Transfer fee and renewal terms are not itemized in publicly available FDD summaries — aggregators flag this as "not separately disclosed." The brand is too young to have an active resale market. Exit value is highly speculative at this stage given limited operating history, no publicly available franchisee revenue data, and franchisor financial instability. A candidate buying into French Florist should plan to hold 5+ years minimum before exit becomes meaningful.

The franchisor is operating at a loss and has negative equity, sustained primarily by a related-party loan from an insider (likely the founder or affiliated entity). This is not uncommon for early-stage franchise systems — growth costs money and franchise fee revenue takes time to stabilize. The $3M strategic investment and the rapid territory sales in 2025 both suggest investor confidence in the unit-level model. However, the franchisor's ability to continue supporting franchisees long-term depends on whether territory sales and royalty revenue can cover operating costs before the loan or investor patience runs out. Every candidate's franchise attorney should review Item 21 and ask the franchisor directly: what does the path to franchisor profitability look like and what is the current runway?

Where French Florist Fits in the FN Pipeline

French Florist is a Zor (franchisor) candidate — a brand FN may want to build a referral relationship with rather than a system to immediately route candidates into. The conversion path is particularly relevant: independent florist owners who are struggling against the national floral networks (Teleflora, 1-800-Flowers) but have an existing customer base and physical shop could convert to French Florist and gain access to their e-commerce infrastructure, centralized marketing, and supply chain. That is a fundamentally different FN use case than sourcing a new franchise investor.

For a candidate with capital to invest in a new franchise unit, French Florist carries too many open questions at this stage — no Item 19, limited franchisee track record, franchisor financial instability — to recommend with confidence. Recommend putting French Florist on a watch list and revisiting when 30+ units are open and franchisee-level revenue data is publicly available.

The Opportunity Lens — What Would Make This a Stronger System
  • Item 19 disclosure: If French Florist adds financial performance representations to the FDD — even a simple median revenue disclosure from their 17 open units — the transparency picture changes dramatically. Ask the franchisor whether Item 19 is planned for a future FDD amendment.
  • Franchisor profitability: If the 2025 audited financials (which would appear in the 2026 FDD update) show the company moving toward breakeven on the strength of territory fees + royalties, that's a materially different risk picture than the 2024 loss.
  • Award-to-open ratio: If the 28 awarded-but-not-yet-open territories begin opening at a normal pace through late 2026, system health improves. If they continue stalling, that's a signal of structural development issues.
  • Conversion path as FN-specific play: If FN has relationships with independent florist owners in Georgia, Texas, or Florida who are looking for an exit or a brand upgrade — the conversion franchise path is a lower-cost, lower-risk entry than a standard franchise and leverages an existing business. This is worth exploring separately from the new-unit investor path.
Due Diligence Questions — Ask French Florist Directly
  • QYou don't include Item 19 in your FDD. Can you share actual revenue and operating income figures from your 17 open franchise locations — not corporate locations? How many of those are in their first year vs. second year of operation?
  • QYour 2024 audited financials show a net loss of over $1.18M and a shareholder deficit. Walk me through the path to franchisor profitability — what does the revenue model look like at 50 open units, and what's your current cash runway?
  • Q45 territories have been awarded but only 17 locations are open. What's happening with the other 28 — are they in build-out, financing, or site selection? What's the typical timeline from territory signing to grand opening?
  • QThe corporate flagship locations are cited as generating 3x the industry average revenue. Are those locations in markets that are representative of where most franchisees will operate, or are they in premium urban markets that skew the comparison?
  • QOn the conversion path — what does the typical conversion franchisee look like? How much of their existing customer base and revenue typically carries over, and what's the average ramp period before they reach the system revenue averages you're citing?
  • QThe $3.1M related-party loan on your balance sheet — what are the terms, and what happens to franchisee support infrastructure if that loan is called or the lender changes terms?
