04Partnership Fit — System Nova Score

Franchise Nova scores any franchise system being considered for candidate (Zee) placement across five dimensions — the System Nova Score. It's normally run against a filed FDD. Jollof Bowl has no FDD yet, so this isn't a precise 0–100 calculation; it's the same five-dimension lens applied honestly to what currently exists, with gaps marked Unscored rather than guessed at.

Standing FN rule: a low or unscored System Nova Score is never a reason to decline a candidate or a partner outright — it means "not yet assessable" or "explore a different structure," which is exactly the framing used below.

What This Actually Tells Us

Run through BLK GRVTY's own lens for evaluating a franchise system, Jollof Bowl comes back Unscored on three of five dimensions and Red on one — not because the underlying business is weak, but because there is, structurally, no franchise system yet for the framework to evaluate. One unit, no FDD, no multi-unit financing terms, no exit mechanics. The one dimension with a real finding today — Operational Model & Ownership Flexibility — points away from FN's multi-unit portfolio thesis as currently structured, since the business is described as needing an owner-operator on site.

This reframes the meeting question again, more specifically than the financial numbers alone suggest: the numbers say this could be a very strong opportunity eventually. BLK GRVTY's own criteria for what makes a good Zor say it isn't one yet — and won't be assessable as one until Jollof Bowl has a real FDD, a documented capital-accessibility structure, and either a path to semi-absentee ownership or an explicit acceptance that this program isn't the multi-unit-portfolio play FN typically routes candidates toward.

05Two Partnership Structures Worth Putting on the Table

Given where Jollof Bowl actually sits against the criteria above, there are two structurally different ways BLK GRVTY could partner with them. Worth deciding as a team which one — or both, sequenced — the meeting should pursue.

Path 1

Future Brand Partner (Zor Type: Brand Partner)

Jollof Bowl becomes a franchise system FN eventually places Zee candidates into, once it clears its own Priority-A gaps: FDD filed, trademark registered, training curriculum built, multi-unit proof of concept established. Zor Stage today would sit at Evaluating Fit, not Active Brand.

Pro — the unit economics and brand momentum are genuinely compelling. This is the upside case.
Con — realistic timeline is longer than the 90–120 days cited in the April assessment once trademark, FDD legal work, and a second unit for proof-of-concept are factored in. Not a near-term placement option for Zees.
Path 2

Treat the Founders as Expansion Operators (Zor Type: Expansion Operator)

Instead of (or ahead of) the Brand Partner track, Andrea Kamara and Lekan Oketunji could run through FN's own Zee pipeline as experienced operators looking to formalize and expand their own units — same intake, same Franchise Nova Stage pipeline used for any candidate, just flagged as already owning a concept.

Pro — available today. Doesn't require Jollof Bowl to have a system ready for placement — uses FN's existing tools to help them close operational and legal gaps faster.
Con — a different value exchange than the Brand Partner model — FN would be investing in Jollof Bowl's readiness rather than gaining a placement pipeline immediately.
These paths aren't mutually exclusive — Path 2 could be the near-term relationship while Path 1 is the long-term one, sequenced explicitly rather than left ambiguous.

07Open Questions for the Meeting

Ask Jollof Bowl Directly

  • What is the actual, current status of the operations manual, training curriculum, and trademark registration — all flagged as high-severity gaps? Timelines for each?
  • Can they produce a Homebase (or equivalent) turnover export and the M&T Bank term sheet? Both resolve unscored metrics quickly.
  • What does a real build-out quote look like, and is the food-hall COGS profile (40.3%) expected to hold in a freestanding footprint, or is 31–32% realistic there?
  • Is there a second unit (brick-and-mortar or food truck) on a real timeline? Multi-unit proof of concept is the biggest lever for both a Priority-A upgrade and a System Health & Growth score.
  • Is the owner-operator requirement flexible at all — could this become a semi-absentee model with a trained GM, or is founder presence structurally required for the food quality/authenticity piece?
  • How dependent is the institutional/catering revenue channel on Andrea Kamara personally, and is there a plan to systematize or delegate it?
  • How would Jollof Bowl feel about an Expansion Operator relationship (going through FN's own program to formalize and grow) as a near-term structure, versus waiting on the Brand Partner path?

Decide Internally as a Team

  • Which partnership path — Brand Partner, Expansion Operator, or both sequenced — does BLK GRVTY want to propose in this meeting?
  • Royalty structure: the file shows at least two different royalty assumptions (5% in an earlier note vs. 6% + 2% marketing fund in the later assessment). Confirm which BLK GRVTY wants to propose.
  • How much weight to give brand/social momentum given it's difficult to independently verify and may not replicate identically in other markets.
  • What BLK GRVTY is willing to commit to help close specific gaps (e.g., connecting them to a franchise attorney for trademark work, or supporting the training curriculum build).

08Partnership Fit — Discussion Framing

On the raw numbers, Jollof Bowl is a strong candidate: franchisee-level ROI clears the Priority-A threshold at every scenario tested, brand demand velocity is real and hard to manufacture, and the founding team's backgrounds are directly relevant to franchise execution.

Run through BLK GRVTY's own System Nova Score lens for evaluating franchise systems, the picture sharpens rather than changes: three of five dimensions are honestly Unscored because there's no franchise system yet to evaluate, and the one dimension with a clear finding today (Operational Model & Ownership Flexibility) points away from FN's multi-unit portfolio thesis as currently structured.

Recommended framing for the meeting: this isn't a single go/no-go on "partnership," it's a decision about which partnership. Use the two paths above to make that decision explicit, and treat today's meeting as the one that sets the timeline and structure — not the one that scores the fit as final.
