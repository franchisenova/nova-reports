01

Bottom line

Viable, but price the diligence in before the unit

RISE lands at 55/100. Semi-absentee ownership is permitted, the franchise fee is modest with real multi-unit discounts, and single-unit territory is genuinely exclusive. Against that: no franchisor financing, an Item 19 built on one affiliate unit, an unaudited balance-sheet-only Item 21, and a disclosure document that is now more than three years old for a system that has since grown to roughly a dozen locations.

Vero Beach generated $506K in 8.5 months (about $59.5K a month) and kept $47.3K as net income, a 9.3% margin. Before interest it produces roughly $69.6K, or 13.7% of revenue. The Bend affiliate in Item 19 ran at about 27% after imputing royalty and fund fees. That gap, plus cash-basis blind spots around prepaid memberships, owner pay, insurance, and loan principal, is where the questions should concentrate.

No candidate is attached to this run, so the capital-gap and candidate-match layers are not scored.

Item 10: no direct or indirect financing and no guarantees. SBA is not restricted. $186.6K–$328.1K of equipment is sole-sourced from the franchisor and non-refundable, which makes the collateral story heavier for a first-time borrower.

Operational Model & Ownership Flexibility

15 / 20

Item 15 permits semi-absentee ownership with a trained GM; the Principal Executive needs only 10% ownership. Points held back for a real compliance load: a licensed medical director, corporate practice of medicine rules, HIPAA, and health-spa contract laws.

Exit & Wealth-Building Structure

5 / 15

$10,000 transfer fee plus broker costs, a franchisor right of first refusal (family transfers excepted), conditional 5-year successor renewals on then-current terms, a 2-year non-compete within 5 miles of any RISE unit, and liquidated damages of up to 2 years of royalty and fund fees. No resale history exists to test the market.

Flags and questions

Stale

The FDD is from June 2023. A franchisor must update annually, and the system has grown well past what this document describes. Every Item 19, 20, and 21 conclusion here should be re-run against the current FDD.

Hidden liability

Prepaid memberships and packages are invisible on a cash basis. Cherry financing (7% of income) and membership sales bring cash in before services are delivered. Request the outstanding package and membership liability from Vagaro.

Under-recorded?

Insurance at $1,129 and medical director at $3,000 YTD look too low for a unit that must carry $1M/$2M general liability, up to $2M malpractice, and workers comp, and must retain a licensed supervising provider. Either annual premiums fall outside this window or costs run through another entity.

No reserve

No depreciation or equipment reserve. $187K–$328K of cryotherapy, sauna, and body-contouring equipment will need service and replacement; the cash-basis P&L sets nothing aside.

Margin lever

Stripe fees run about 2.9% of services. At this volume a processor renegotiation or ACH push for memberships is worth a few thousand dollars a year.

Inconsistency

The FDD contradicts itself on franchised outlet counts (Item 20 Tables 1 vs 3 and 5), minimum royalties (cover risk page vs Item 6), and MUDA totals (cover vs Item 7). Ask the franchisor to reconcile in writing.

Minor

An NSF fee of $280 suggests at least one cash-timing miss this year. Small, but worth asking about alongside the loan schedules.
