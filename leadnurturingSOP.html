Lead Nurturing SOP

How a lead moves from first contact through nurture, engagement, or closure — and what starts, stops, and restarts along the way.

1. Definitions

BG Lead Status

One property answers "where does this person stand with us right now," regardless of which initiative they belong to.

Active –
Nurturing		 In an automated nurture sequence, hasn't responded yet. Set automatically when someone enters nurture.
Engaged They've responded, or a person is actively in conversation with them. A human owns the follow-up — no automated emails go out while someone is Engaged.
Lost Lead –
Unresponsive		 Went through a full nurture sequence and never responded. Not permanent — attending a future event puts them back into nurture.

Lead vs. In the Pipeline

StageDefinition
LeadA connection we're nurturing — has not yet completed an intake form.
In the PipelineHas completed an intake form (or, for certain partner-side records, been manually entered). This is the only trigger that moves someone from Lead to Pipeline.
Partner-side records (organizations or systems we work with directly, rather than individual candidates): the intake form isn't built for these. If real work is happening with one of these without a formal intake/agreement step, manually add them to the pipeline at whatever stage reflects the actual work underway.
One-off / informal engagements (e.g. connecting a candidate directly to a capital partner without the full program): going forward, everyone should go through an intake form unless it's a genuine one-off. One-offs still get placed into the pipeline at whatever stage they're actually at.

2. How a lead enters nurture

There are three ways in. Each behaves differently on purpose.

Path A — They registered for an event

Everyone who registers for an event enters the nurture sequence for that event's initiative, 7 days after the event. Whether or not they actually showed up doesn't change this — registering is enough of a signal to keep the conversation going.

The one exception: anyone already marked Engaged is skipped. A live conversation is never interrupted by an automated sequence.

Path B — Added manually from a call or email

Someone we met, spoke with, or emailed who wasn't tied to an event. A real conversation is already underway, so these leads do not go into nurture — automated "nice to meet you" emails landing on top of a live thread undercut the relationship.

1
Create the Contact with name, email, and phone.
2
Set BG Initiative based on whatever conversation led to them being added.
3
Set BG Lead Status to Engaged. You own the follow-up from here — no automation will run for them.
Engaged is not a parking spot. Marking someone Engaged means a person is responsible for them. Section 5's weekly review exists specifically to catch Engaged contacts that have gone quiet, so nobody sits untouched for months.

Path C — Re-entry after a new event

Someone previously marked Lost Lead – Unresponsive who shows up at another event gets a second run — automatically, in the nurture sequence for whichever initiative that new event belonged to.

The one exception: if they're currently Engaged, nothing happens. A live conversation is never interrupted by an automated sequence, even if they attend something new.

3. What happens during nurture

Each initiative has its own nurture sequence — its own emails, its own cadence, its own voice. What's identical across all of them is the surrounding logic:

  • On entry, BG Lead Status is set to Active – Nurturing
  • The sequence runs its emails on schedule
  • At any point, engaging pulls them out immediately (Section 4)
  • If the sequence finishes with no engagement, they're marked Lost Lead – Unresponsive
Nobody is ever in two nurture sequences at once. Entering one sets Active – Nurturing; engaging exits it entirely.

4. How nurture stops — engagement

One workflow watches for engagement across every initiative and sets BG Lead Status to Engaged. Every nurture sequence has a single unenrollment trigger tied to that status, so the moment someone engages, all automated email stops.

SignalHow it's caught
Replies to one of our emailsAutomatic
Submits the intake form or another engagement formAutomatic
Calls, texts, or talks to us in personManual — whoever hears from them sets BG Lead Status to Engaged
The manual one matters most. Phone calls, texts, and hallway conversations leave no trace HubSpot can detect. If you hear from someone by any channel and don't set their status, they'll keep receiving automated emails as though they'd never responded — which is exactly the impression we don't want to give.
Registration and check-in forms are deliberately excluded from engagement detection. They're forms, but submitting one means someone signed up for an event — not that they responded to us. Counting them would mark every registrant as Engaged and stop nurture before it ever starts.

5. Weekly review — Engaged leads that have gone quiet

Engaged contacts have no automation running for them, by design. That protection becomes a liability if the person responsible gets busy — which is how leads historically went months without contact.

1
Open the "Engaged – No Recent Activity" dashboard view. It surfaces anyone marked Engaged with no activity in 14+ days.
2
For each one, decide: reach out personally, or hand them back to automation by changing BG Lead Status to Active – Nurturing and enrolling them in the right initiative's nurture sequence.
3
If the relationship is genuinely over, mark them Lost Lead – Unresponsive so they're not counted as live.
This view should stay short. A growing one means leads are being marked Engaged and then forgotten — the exact problem this system exists to prevent.

6. Lost leads

Lost Lead – Unresponsive means someone completed a full nurture sequence without ever responding. It's a status, not a deletion — they keep their Contact record, their BG Initiative tags, and their full history.

Nothing is permanent. Attending any future event puts a lost lead straight back into nurture for that event's initiative. People's timing changes; the system assumes a "no" is about right now, not forever.

7. Capital Consortium — status detail

StatusMeaning
LeadInitial contact made; interest not yet confirmed.

Capital partners are tracked as Contacts with BG Initiative set to Capital Consortium. Because it's its own initiative, Capital Consortium gets its own nurture workflow — separate emails, separate cadence, written for capital partners rather than candidates.

8. When a Lead becomes a Deal

A candidate becomes a Deal in HubSpot when they sign the participation agreement. The signed agreement is what creates the Deal record, and each contact gets added to a deal pipeline depending on the BG Initiative.
