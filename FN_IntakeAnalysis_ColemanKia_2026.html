Est. Investment = total cost to open one location (franchise fee + build-out + equipment + working capital) — not what you earn.

B2C = your customers are individual consumers. B2B = your customers are other businesses.

Territory Protection — Most franchise agreements grant a ‘protected’ or ‘exclusive’ territory. In plain terms: this stops the franchisor from opening another location of the same brand near you. It does not stop a different brand, or an independent business, from opening nearby — no franchise agreement can do that. Some agreements also carve out exceptions even within your territory (online sales, national accounts, other brands under the same parent company). We’ve noted what’s publicly known for each system below; always confirm the exact terms with the franchisor.

Market Saturation — A measure of how crowded a market already is, and how much of the local customer base a new location would need to capture just to break even. It matters most for businesses people visit out of habit or convenience — too many similar options nearby make it hard for any one location to stand out. It matters far less for businesses where customers are referred through an event, like an insurance claim after storm damage.

● Low — demand isn’t driven by nearby competition● Moderate — some competitive pressure, but manageable● High — a crowded, competitive category; go in with eyes open

Revenue Type — Whether a location earns from the same customers coming back (recurring) or from a steady stream of new, one-time jobs (one-time/project). Recurring sounds better on paper, but it isn’t automatically the safer bet — it often comes with more upfront equipment and real estate, which takes longer to earn back.

Payback Period — How long it typically takes an owner to earn back their total investment, based on public estimates from each system’s own disclosed costs and earnings. This is different from break-even, which is when a location starts turning a monthly profit — a location can be profitable month-to-month within a year and still take five more years to pay back what it cost to open. We’ve included public payback estimates below because they’re one of the clearest ways to compare systems apples-to-apples.
