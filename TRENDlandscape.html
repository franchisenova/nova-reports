About This List

This list ranks franchise systems that could lease space in TREND's neighborhood shopping centers, with a grant covering about half of what families pay. It favors brands that are affordable to open, fit a small storefront, and charge a monthly fee that's easy to split between a grant and a family. Rankings change by location, because each center's neighborhood is different.

Est. Investment — total cost to open one location: franchise fee, build-out, equipment, working capital. Not expected income.

Family Cost After 50% Grant — what a family would pay each month if a grant covers half of typical tuition. Tuition is set by each local owner, so these are estimates.

Royalty Minimum — some brands charge a set monthly royalty even when revenue is low. That matters most in the first year, while enrollment is still building.

Item 19 — the section of a franchise's disclosure document where it can publish what its locations earn. Brands that skip it are harder for lenders to underwrite.

Market Saturation — how crowded the category already is. For tutoring and enrichment, this means how many similar centers families can already reach nearby.

● Low — few similar options nearby ● Moderate — some competition; confirm territory ● High — a crowded category

⚑ Based on publicly available information only — we have not reviewed the Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) for any of these systems. Neighborhood figures are Census estimates (ACS 2023) for each center's ZIP code. Confirm territory availability and exact terms directly with each franchisor.

Kumon Math & Reading
Education

Self-paced math and reading in a small storefront, with monthly tuition per subject. It has the lowest cost to open on this list, and the lowest price for families: most centers charge about $150–$200 per subject a month, so a 50% grant brings one subject to roughly $75–$100. The trade-off is the royalty: Kumon charges a flat fee per student rather than a percentage, so if a center prices lower to fit the neighborhood, the royalty takes a bigger share of each dollar. Kumon also doesn't publish revenue data, which makes lenders work harder to underwrite it.

After-school math tutoring with a membership-style monthly fee. Making the 50% Grant Work
Pay the center, not the family. Ohio gave families $1,000 per child for tutoring and enrichment through its ACE accounts, but most of the money went unused and the program wasn't renewed, partly because parents struggled to get claims paid. A grant that pays the center directly avoids that problem.
Fit the grant to the royalty. Brands with a monthly royalty minimum (Mathnasium, Huntington) need steady enrollment from the start. A grant committed for the first 12–18 months helps a new location get through ramp-up.
To confirm with TREND: available bay sizes at each center, rent, and whether two concepts could share a center (for example, tutoring next to a pet groomer at Edmondson Village).
