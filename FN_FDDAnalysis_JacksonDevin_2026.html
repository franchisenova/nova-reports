FN Recommendation
Build the Tallahassee Pilot Store first  ·  Hold Area Development until it has 12 months of results

Devin told us his goal is "regional or national expansion" with "autolab" in "north florida." It appears he has already taken the first step by securing the Northwest Florida Area Developer rights. The FN recommendation is to sequence that ambition, not slow it down. The Pilot Store at 200 N. Magnolia is the foundation for everything else: the ADA requires it, it becomes the training site for every future Florida franchisee, and its results are the only proof of Auto-Lab's Florida economics anyone will have, since the franchisor discloses no Item 19. The existing 4,900 SF automotive building, his 780+ credit, and his $1M+ net worth make the single-unit capital stack clearly closeable. The $50K loan he mentioned just needs to be sized up to fit the real cost to open.

The Area Developer layer scores 19/100 on its own merits. The AD network shrank from 3 to 2 in 2025. The service-fee basis is inconsistent between the FDD and the ADA. Territory rights are non-exclusive. The ADA and store agreement cross-default, so trouble in one puts both at risk. None of this is a reason to walk away. It is a reason to use FN's leverage now: get the service fee confirmed in writing, try to start the development-schedule clock after the Pilot Store stabilizes, and hold off on franchise recruiting until the Pilot Store has a year of operating data. The franchisor's thin balance sheet (2025 loss, about $73K cash) makes this sequencing more important. Devin's regional plan should not depend on Auto-Lab's capacity to support fast Florida growth.

What FN Can Unlock for Devin
  • SBA lender packaging built on his 780+ credit and $1M+ net worth: an upsized 7(a) for the Pilot Store, vendor equipment financing, and an SBA 504 path if he is buying the Magnolia building rather than leasing it.
  • A franchisor diligence request on Devin's behalf: written service-fee clarification, corrected Item 20 tables and Exhibit H name, the signed ADA development schedule, and SBA Directory status. These are asks a first-time operator rarely gets answered alone.
  • Structured validation calls with Michigan operators (including a newer store) and the Indiana and Central Texas Area Developers, standing in for the missing Item 19.
  • An operator-talent plan: identifying an experienced service manager or lead technician for Tallahassee, which is the biggest lever given Devin's first-time-operator profile.
Risks to Watch
  • No Item 19 in either FDD. There is no disclosed sales, margin, or AD-income data for a system operating since 1989. Every financial projection for Tallahassee will rest on validation calls, not disclosure. Treat any verbal numbers from the franchisor as something to document and verify.
  • Franchisor financial capacity. 2025 net loss, negative operating cash flow, $54.6K equity, distributions exceeding income, a $43K advance to the majority member, and a new CEO as of June 2026. Florida support will come from a small Michigan team. Ask for a written support plan.
  • Cross-default between the ADA and the Pilot Store. A default on either agreement can terminate both. Combined with $50K liquidated damages per undeveloped store, an aggressive development schedule is the biggest structural exposure. Nova Guide needs the signed ADA schedule.
  • Capital picture is incomplete. Monthly debt obligations were not provided, whether the AD fee was paid from the $250K is unknown, and it isn't clear whether "purchased a location" means owning the building (listed at $1.6M) or leasing it (~$12,250/mo + NNN). Resolve all three before lender conversations.
  • First-time operator in a technical trade. No prior business-operating or executive sales experience per intake, a $450K minimum-sales hurdle from year two, and a Pilot Store that must double as the Florida training site. The service-manager hire is make-or-break.
