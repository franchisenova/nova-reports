Industry Read — Market, Economy & Public Knowledge Beyond the FDD
Laundromat demand
IBISWorld estimates U.S. laundromat revenue at about $7.2B in 2026, growing roughly 1.6% a year over five years. Elevated utility prices and wage pressure are squeezing profit. Growth is coming from wash-dry-fold and pickup-and-delivery, which both WaveMAX and Speed Queen offer. Secondary
IBISWorld; PlanetLaundry
Atlanta renter base
About 54% of City of Atlanta households rent, and metro Atlanta has about 6.4M residents. Rent burden is high: 29.3% of metro renters spend more than half their income on rent — the price-sensitive, no-in-unit-laundry customer laundromats serve. Rents were roughly flat through 2025. Secondary
RentCafe; CoStar via Matthews; Axios Atlanta
Georgia utility costs
Georgia Power bills rose more than 20% after five increases from 2023 to 2025. Base rates are now frozen through 2028, but fuel costs and Hurricane Helene recovery (about $830M+) are being handled separately and could still raise bills. Utilities run about 11–15% of revenue in Speed Queen's company stores, so this is a real line item. Secondary Computed
Georgia PSC / Georgia Power; Zacks; AJC
Interest rates
WSJ Prime has been 6.75% since December 2025. That puts Speed Queen's parent financing at about 8.25–11.25% and SBA 7(a) loans over $350K at up to 9.75% (prime + 3%). Every laundry deal in this report is a leveraged deal. Secondary Computed
Bankrate; SBA 7(a) rate caps
Speed Queen's parent went public
Alliance Laundry Holdings, which makes Speed Queen machines and lends to franchisees, listed on the NYSE (ALH) in October 2025 and raised $826M, largely to pay down about $2B of debt. It holds roughly 40% of the North American commercial laundry market, and BDT remains its principal holder. A public, well-capitalized parent supports the lender relationship, even though the franchise entity itself runs a deficit. Secondary
SEC 8-K; IPOScoop
WaveMAX in the press
WaveMAX fell from #416 (2024) to #456 (2025) to #485 (2026) on Entrepreneur's Franchise 500. It has a dedicated Area Director for Georgia and Tennessee, which fits the FDD's note that about 48% of royalties go to area representatives. That area director would be KC's main support contact. Secondary
Franchise Chatter; 1851 Franchise

FN Recommendation for KC
Lead: WaveMAX (mid-size build) · Conditional: Speed Queen

WaveMAX is KC's lead laundry system, at the right size. Atlanta's large renter base and WaveMAX's thin Georgia footprint (1 unit) give her room to pick a strong site, and her real estate background is a real edge there. The key is keeping the build around $600K–$900K. At the $431K median, a full $1.6M build financed by debt would spend more than half of revenue on loan payments.

Speed Queen is the better-run brand but the wrong size for KC right now. Its disclosure, parent lender, and Georgia company stores are the best in this comparison. But a traditional store's loan payments exceed mid-tier store earnings. It only works at a top-tier site, with more cash than she listed, or if Small Footprint stores start producing results.

Across both industries: laundry needs about 2–5 times more capital than restoration, and its returns depend on the site and the loan rather than on KC's selling. If she wants to use her lending and investor network as an owner-operator, restoration (DRYmedic) fits her better. If she wants an asset-backed, manager-run business, laundry does.

What would change this call
  • KC is willing to put in substantially more cash, or to buy the real estate → Speed Queen becomes viable at a strong Atlanta site.
  • WaveMAX can't explain its 52 unopened agreements or show franchisee cost data → step back and compare against independent laundromat acquisitions (like the Norcross resale on her landscape).
  • Rates fall meaningfully → debt coverage improves for both, most of all Speed Queen.
Risks to Watch — For KC
  • Leverage: Laundry returns depend on debt terms; at mid-2026 rates, oversized builds don't cover their loan payments at median revenue.
  • Utility exposure: Hurricane Helene storm-cost recovery and fuel pass-throughs could raise Georgia Power bills even with base rates frozen.
  • Site risk: Rent and utilities vary block to block; a weak Atlanta site can't be fixed with sales effort the way a restoration territory can.
  • WaveMAX backlog: 52 signed-but-unopened agreements could mean stalled deals or financing problems — ask before signing.
  • Speed Queen hidden costs: Item 7 excludes real estate and lists only $20K–$40K of working capital for a 6–18 month build-out.
