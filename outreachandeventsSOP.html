1. Definitions

Everyone and everything BG comes into contact with falls into one of these buckets. Get the event setup in Section 2 right, and these numbers take care of themselves.

BG
Initiative		 Which of BG's seven initiatives a lead belongs to — Franchise Nova, BG Enterprise, Wealth Through Women's Wellbeing, Refuge, Preservation Home & Land, Equipped, or Proximate Leaders. Set on every Contact the moment they come in.
Direct Participants
(Count)		 A team member is speaking, presenting, or moderating — a webinar, panel, or talk — where we know a specific number of people were reached, but did not capture individual names or contact info. Entered as one number on the Marketing Event.
Direct Participants
(Confirmed)		 We captured a name + email — through registration, a check-in form, a QR code scan, or a list we imported. This is not a typed-in number: it's everyone marked Attended on the Marketing Event, which HubSpot counts for you.
Indirect
Participants		 People present at an event we had a presence at, but who we can't confirm any direct engagement with — the rest of a conference floor, a room's total headcount beyond who we actually spoke with. Entered as one number on the Marketing Event, separate from Direct Count.
Capital
Consortium		 Banks and capital partners in our referral network for candidate capital access. Tracked at the Company level by status — not counted alongside participant metrics. See Section 9 for the full status list.
Not tracked at all: we had no formal presence at an event — attending as a guest, wandering a conference floor with no booth or speaking role. No aggregate number gets logged. Anyone individually engaged in that setting still gets entered as a Contact.
Registered ≠ attended: registering alone doesn't make someone a Direct Participant. Only people marked Attended on the Marketing Event count toward Direct Participants (Confirmed) — registrants who never show up stay at Registered, which automatically excludes them from that number while still keeping them as a real Contact you can follow up with.

2. Every event starts here — create the Marketing Event

Every single event gets a Marketing Event record — whether we host it, someone else hosts it, or we're just attending. No exceptions. This is the one place all event data lives.

1
Create the Marketing Event and name it using this exact format every time: EVENT NAME - DATE. Consistent naming is what makes events findable and reportable later.
Event coordinator
2
Fill in every field:
  • Status — Pending
  • Description — what the event is, and anything worth knowing at a glance
  • Organizer — who's running it (us or the host organization)
  • Event Type — see the list below
  • Start Date and End Date
  • Partnered Event — Yes or No
  • Partner Name(s) — leave blank if Partnered Event is No
HubSpot property
Event Type — use one of these: Conference · Webinar · Workshop or Reception · Speaking Engagement · Capital Consortium Event · Other

7. Keeping everything in one place — notes & attachments

The Marketing Event is the natural home for everything about that event, not just the numbers. This matters twice: during the event, so anyone who needs something knows where to find it — and later, when you're planning a similar event and want to see what it actually took last time.

Worth adding as notes: the agenda or run of show · event budget · booth location and number · staffing schedule and who's covering what · speaker names and bios · vendor or catering details · shipping and tracking for booth materials · lessons learned afterward
Worth adding as attachments: venue or floor maps · slide decks · marketing materials and one-pagers · sponsor or vendor agreements · signed contracts · photos from the event

9. Leads vs. the Pipeline

StageDefinition
LeadA connection we're now nurturing — has not yet completed an intake form.
In the PipelineHas completed an intake form (or, for certain partner-side records — see below — been manually entered). This is the only trigger that moves someone from Lead to Pipeline.
Partner-side records (organizations or systems we work with directly, rather than individual candidates): the intake form isn't built for these. If real work is happening with one of these without a formal intake/agreement step, manually add them to the pipeline at whatever stage reflects the actual work underway.
One-off / informal engagements (e.g. connecting a candidate directly to a capital partner without the full program): going forward, everyone should go through an intake form unless it's a genuine one-off. One-offs still get placed into the pipeline at whatever stage they're actually at.
Re-engaging with a different BG initiative mid-nurture: if a contact is still at Lead when they engage with a different initiative, their current nurture pauses and they're flagged for a human decision. If they're already further along, the new initiative just gets added to their record and their existing progress continues untouched.

10. When a Lead becomes a Deal

A candidate becomes a Deal in HubSpot when they sign the participation agreement. The signed agreement is what creates the Deal record, and each contact gets added to a deal pipeline depending on the BG Initiative.

Note: events are no longer tracked as deals — that's what Marketing Events replaced. Deals are now used only for candidates who've signed a participation agreement.
