Industry Read — Market, Economy & Public Knowledge Beyond the FDD
Restoration demand
Market-size estimates vary widely, from about $7.1B to $80B in the U.S., with growth commonly put at 4–6% a year. Water mitigation is still the largest revenue driver, but its margins have tightened year over year. Secondary
Restoration & Remediation magazine, 2025 Year in Review
Cash flow reality
Most restoration contractors wait 3–8 weeks for insurance payments. An owner-operator needs working capital to carry jobs; the FDD working-capital lines (DRYmedic $32K–$56K; PuroClean $20K–$35K) may be thin for a busy first year. Secondary Computed
Cleanfax 2026 data via WebTonic

PuroClean in the press
PuroClean was named a 2026 Entrepreneur Franchise 500 top-100 brand, a Franchise Business Review 2026 Top Franchise, and an Entrepreneur Top 10 Maintenance franchise. It promotes about 500 locations; the FDD counts 433 U.S. franchised outlets, so the public figure likely includes non-U.S. units. It says 20% of 2026 unit growth came from existing owners adding territories. None of the press coverage mentions the pending franchisee lawsuit. Secondary
Franchising.com; IFA; PR Newswire
Georgia licensing
Georgia requires a general contractor license for projects over $2,500, but no separate mold-remediation license. A 2025 overhaul created a new licensing board with exams, financial-responsibility proof, continuing education, and two years of supervised experience for residential-basic licenses. KC has no construction background, so reconstruction work would need a licensed qualifier or subcontractors. Secondary
Restoration Industry Association; R&R state licensing roundup
Atlanta market fit
Metro Atlanta has about 6.4M residents and has been among the top U.S. metros for population growth, with roughly 54% of city households renting. Large rental and investor-owned housing stock means property managers and investors are a real referral channel — the exact network KC built through KC Horizons. Secondary
CoStar via Matthews; RentCafe

Item 6 — Ongoing Fee Burden Amber
Royalty 7% / 6% / 5% · Brand fund 1.5% stepping down · $54K/yr required local marketing
Royalty is 7% on the first $1M of mitigation/contents revenue, 6% to $5M, 5% above, resetting every January; reconstruction revenue pays 3%. Minimum royalty: none months 1–6, then $900, $1,500, and $2,625/month from month 49. Brand fund is the greater of $595 or 1.5% in months 7–24, then 1.5% stepping down to 0% above $10M. Local marketing: $13,500 in the first three months, then $54,000 per calendar year ($30K directed to designated SEO/digital/direct-mail vendors, $350/month website fee counts toward it, up to $18K can fund a commercial business-development hire). Technology: DASH $700/month plus $100/month tech fee (can rise to $500+). Core royalty plus fund is ~8.5%; with required marketing and tech it is ~26% of revenue at the $363K median territory and ~21% at $500K. Primary Computed

Items 11 & 15 — Ownership Model Amber
Owner-operator on unit one · Key Person must be an owner · Sales/marketing hire required by month 6
First-unit Key Person must be an owner and work on premises; a non-owner Key Person is allowed only on second and later franchises. Training is 8 hours online plus about 6 business days of classroom (48 classroom + 48 hands-on hours). Typical open is 3–6 months after signing and must happen within 90 days of finishing training. A full-time employee dedicated to sales and marketing is required from month 6 for the life of the agreement. Contents and reconstruction services require approval after 12 months of operation plus extra equipment, vehicles, training and insurance. Not a semi-absentee model at entry. Primary
Item 12 — Territory Amber
Protected, not exclusive · up to 250K population · Revenue minimums of $300K (yr 3) and $450K (yr 4+)
Territory is zip-code defined, capped at 400 sq mi and 50 miles across. "Protected" means no other DRYmedic/STOP operator may actively market to homeowners and local businesses in it — but others may still solicit insurance adjusters, corporate HQs, and property managers there, which are the core referral channels in restoration. Miss the minimums and the franchisor can impose a revenue improvement program, then shrink the territory or terminate. Out-of-territory work triggers escalating infringement fees (25%, 50%, then 100% of the job's revenue) and termination on the fourth violation. Primary

FN Recommendation for KC
Lead: DRYmedic · Hold: PuroClean pending answers

DRYmedic is KC's lead restoration system. Atlanta strengthens the case: the parent company is moving its headquarters to Cobb County, Georgia is one of its newest growth markets, and the mitigation-first model lets KC open without a Georgia contractor license. Her capital is more than enough, and the debt load is light at about 14% of a median territory's revenue. The work is real: she has to sell from day one, carry heavy required marketing, and hit $300K by year three. Her lending and investor network is the right starting point for that.

PuroClean stays on hold. On paper it fits KC's capital and owner-operator plan just as well. But in Georgia it shrank from 15 units to 12, it has open territories in a crowded market, and the franchisor issues raised in the FDD (litigation, owner loans, falling earnings) aren't reflected in its strong public rankings. Revisit once those are answered or if a Georgia resale or conversion opportunity comes up.

What would change this call
  • DRYmedic shows no open Atlanta-area territory → look at the two-hour ring or a DRYmedic resale through Authority Brands.
  • PuroClean resolves the Born Active claim and explains its Georgia closures → reopen as a lower-cost alternative.
  • KC decides she wants a manager-run rather than owner-operated business → laundry becomes the stronger industry for her.
Risks to Watch — For KC
  • Sales ramp: DRYmedic's bottom-quartile territories average about $117K, far below the $300K year-3 minimum. KC's referral network has to convert early.
  • Insurance cash cycle: 3–8 week payment delays plus heavy required marketing can strain working capital; plan beyond the FDD's three-month estimate.
  • Territory overlap: Insurance adjusters and property managers — KC's natural channel — can be solicited by other DRYmedic operators in her territory.
  • Parent leverage: Authority Brands has lost money three years running and carries $542M of debt. The move to Atlanta is positive but doesn't change that.
  • Licensing creep: Georgia's 2025 licensing overhaul raises the bar if she moves into reconstruction; budget for a licensed partner.
