FN Capital Readiness Assessment
Fundable at a right-sized amount — not at $3M, and not yet

The core business is real and improving: revenue nearly doubled in 2025, 2026 is on pace to grow again, margins hold near 60%, overhead is low, and most revenue is backed by third-party records. On today's numbers it could likely support a loan somewhere in the low-to-mid hundreds of thousands, comfortably covering the original $150K plan for three locations.

The $3M ask is roughly four to ten times what current cash flow can carry, and BG works on the debt side only. Before lender introductions, three things need to change: books a lender will accept, a set owner salary so cash flow is measurable, and an answer to the key-person question. A phased ask (first one or two locations, then grow from results) is the more fundable story.

What FN Can Unlock Here
  • A clear, right-sized ask tied to a use-of-funds plan, rerun once Justin sends the pre-call items
  • A short fix list for him: bookkeeping software and a bookkeeper, cash recorded at time of sale, a set owner salary
  • Introductions to smaller-ticket lenders in the Capital Consortium once the books are cleaned up
Risks to Watch
  • Ask far exceeds capacity. If Justin anchors on $3M, lender conversations will stall. The number needs to be reset before any introduction.
  • Books won't hold up to underwriting yet. Cash-basis, owner-prepared records with missing fees and after-the-fact cash entries will be discounted or questioned.
  • Key-person dependence. One part-time payroll line suggests the business runs on Justin. Lenders will want a backup plan.
  • Entity confusion. Two LLCs and an all-zero franchisor balance sheet need a clear explanation before a lender sees the FDD.
  • Short track record of documented numbers. Nine months of detailed P&L plus two summary years. Lenders will verify against tax returns and bank statements, which Justin should have ready for them directly.
